# Diabetes foot care

## About
Diabetes can reduce feeling in the feet and slow healing. Small cuts or blisters can become serious infections, so daily foot care is very important.

## Self-care
Every day:
- Look at both feet every day, including between the toes and the soles. Use a mirror or ask a family member.
- Wash feet daily in lukewarm water (test the temperature with your elbow), and dry carefully between the toes.
- Moisturise dry skin, but not between the toes.
- Cut toenails straight across. Do not cut corns or calluses yourself.
- Always wear well-fitting footwear, even at home. Check shoes for stones before wearing.
- Never walk barefoot, and never use hot water bottles or heating pads on your feet.
- Keep your blood sugar within the target your doctor has set.

## See a doctor if
- You find a cut, blister, crack, corn or colour change that is not healing within 2 days.
- You notice new numbness, tingling or burning in the feet.
- An ingrown toenail, or swelling of the foot or ankle.
- You have not had a full foot check in the last 12 months.

## Emergency signs - call 112 / 108
- A wound with spreading redness, pus, a bad smell, or black skin.
- A hot, red, swollen foot with fever.
- Very high or very low blood sugar with confusion, drowsiness, vomiting or fainting.
