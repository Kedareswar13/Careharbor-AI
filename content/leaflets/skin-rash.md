# Skin rash and itching

## About
Rashes have many causes: heat (prickly heat), sweat and fungal infections, allergies to soaps or food, insect bites, eczema or viral illnesses. Many settle with simple care.

## Self-care
- Keep the skin cool, clean and dry. Wear loose cotton clothing.
- Bathe with lukewarm water and a mild, fragrance-free soap. Pat dry, especially in skin folds.
- Moisturise dry skin with a plain, fragrance-free moisturiser.
- Avoid scratching; keep nails short.
- Stop any new soap, detergent, cosmetic or hair dye that may have caused the rash.
- Do not apply steroid creams or home remedies without advice; they can make fungal rashes worse.

## See a doctor if
- The rash lasts more than a week, or keeps spreading.
- The rash is painful, blistering, or has pus or crusting.
- You have a fever with the rash.
- A ring-shaped itchy rash in the groin, armpits or between the toes is not improving.
- The rash started after a new medicine (keep the medicine name to show the doctor).

## Emergency signs - call 112 / 108
- Swelling of the lips, tongue, face or throat, or difficulty breathing (severe allergy).
- A rash of purple-red spots that does not fade when pressed with a glass, especially with fever.
- Widespread blistering or peeling skin, or sores in the mouth and eyes.
- Feeling faint or dizzy with hives.
