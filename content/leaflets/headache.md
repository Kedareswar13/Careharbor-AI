# Headache

## About
Most headaches are tension-type headaches caused by stress, poor sleep, skipped meals, dehydration, long screen time or poor posture. Migraines are throbbing headaches, often on one side, sometimes with nausea or sensitivity to light. Most headaches are not dangerous.

## Self-care
- Drink a glass or two of water and eat something if you have skipped a meal.
- Rest in a quiet, dark room. A cool cloth on the forehead or neck can help.
- Take regular screen breaks: every 20 minutes look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds.
- Gently stretch your neck and shoulders. Sit with your back supported.
- Keep regular sleep and meal times. Limit tea and coffee late in the day.
- Use a pain reliever only as advised by your doctor or pharmacist. Using pain relievers on more than 10 days a month can itself cause headaches.
- Keep a headache diary: time, food, sleep and triggers.

## See a doctor if
- Headaches happen more than once a week or are getting worse over weeks.
- The headache wakes you from sleep or is worst on waking with vomiting.
- You are over 50 and this is a new kind of headache.
- Headaches come with vision changes, or started after a minor head injury.
- Your usual medicines no longer help.

## Emergency signs - call 112 / 108
- A sudden, severe "worst ever" or "thunderclap" headache that peaks within a minute.
- Headache with stiff neck and fever, or with a purple rash.
- Face drooping, arm or leg weakness, slurred speech, confusion or loss of vision.
- Headache after a significant head injury, or with fainting or fits.
- Severe headache in pregnancy, especially with swelling or blurred vision.
