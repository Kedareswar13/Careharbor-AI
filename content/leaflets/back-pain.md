# Lower back pain

## About
Lower back pain is very common, especially with desk work, lifting or long commutes. Most back pain is from muscles and ligaments and improves within a few weeks.

## Self-care
- Stay as active as you can. Short walks are better than bed rest.
- Use a warm pack or hot water bottle (wrapped in a towel) for 15–20 minutes.
- Sit with your back supported and feet flat. Stand up and move every 30–45 minutes.
- Lift by bending your knees, keeping the load close to your body. Avoid twisting while lifting.
- Sleep on your side with a pillow between your knees, or on your back with a pillow under your knees.
- Gentle stretches once the pain eases. Our physiotherapist can teach a safe routine.

## See a doctor if
- The pain is not improving after 2 weeks, or keeps coming back.
- Pain spreads down a leg below the knee, or there is tingling or numbness in the leg.
- The pain is worse at night or when lying down.
- You also have fever, unexplained weight loss, or a history of cancer or osteoporosis.

## Emergency signs - call 112 / 108
- Numbness around the genitals or buttocks ("saddle" area).
- New difficulty passing urine, or loss of bladder or bowel control.
- Sudden weakness in both legs.
- Back pain after a major fall or accident.
- Severe back or tummy pain with fainting or sweating.
