# Fever in adults

## About
A fever is a body temperature of 100.4 °F (38 °C) or higher. Most fevers in adults are caused by viral infections and settle within 2–3 days. A fever is the body's normal way of fighting infection.

## Self-care
- Rest and drink plenty of fluids: water, oral rehydration solution, coconut water, soups or buttermilk.
- Wear light cotton clothing and keep the room airy. Avoid heavy blankets.
- Sponge the forehead, armpits and neck with lukewarm (not cold) water.
- Check and note your temperature every 4–6 hours.
- Only use a fever medicine your doctor or pharmacist has advised, exactly as written on the label. Do not combine several cold-and-flu products.
- Eat light, easy-to-digest food such as khichdi, idli or rice with dal.

## See a doctor if
- The fever lasts more than 3 days, or keeps coming back.
- The temperature is 103 °F (39.4 °C) or higher.
- You have a painful sore throat, ear pain, burning urine, a cough with yellow or green phlegm, or a new rash.
- You have recently travelled, or there is dengue, malaria or typhoid in your area.
- You have diabetes, kidney disease, cancer treatment or another condition that weakens immunity.

## Emergency signs - call 112 / 108
- Stiff neck with fever, severe headache, or confusion.
- Difficulty breathing, chest pain, or bluish lips.
- Fainting, fits (seizures) or extreme drowsiness.
- A rash of small purple-red spots that does not fade when pressed with a glass.
- Bleeding from the gums or nose, or black stools during a fever.
- Any fever in a baby under 3 months old.
